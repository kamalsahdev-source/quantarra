How Discovery Works Discovery is not a questionnaire the bank fills in from memory. The platform connects to the bank's own systems with read-only access, scans them, classifies the data it finds, and drafts the profile from what it can prove. The bank's job is to confirm or correct, never to start from a blank page.

1Connect read-onlyThe bank connects its systems with read-only credentials. Discovery can look; it can never write to, change, or delete anything in a source system.
›
2Scan the systemsDaily scans inventory accounts, cloud resources, changes, and configurations, and diff them against the previous scan and the approved baseline. New systems and drift surface on their own.
›
3Classify the dataData stores are profiled and classified by content: customer PII, transaction and monitoring data, identity data, telemetry. What is absent is recorded too: no PHI and no cardholder data anywhere in the estate.
›
4Pre-fill with provenanceProfile answers are drafted from what discovery found. Every answer carries its source: the document and page, the connected system, or the scan, with the date it was seen.
›
5Bank confirmsNothing discovery proposes becomes the bank's profile on its own. A named person confirms or corrects each answer, and the confirmation is logged against the profile.
Corroboration
Answers are checked across sources, not taken from one
Where the bank and its data operate is established three ways: the charter record, the regions on the AWS accounts, and the Entra ID tenant. When sources disagree, discovery raises a conflict for the bank to resolve instead of averaging the answers away. One is open now: the Data Retention working draft conflicts with approved v2.4, so the retention answer below stays unconfirmed until the CCO resolves it.
Confidence states
Every answer carries a state the bank can see
Confirmed, Pre-filled · needs review, or Stale. An answer goes stale when the scan or extract behind it ages past its freshness rule: the BSA disposition extract behind the monitoring answers is stale today (Oct 30, 2026), and the profile says so rather than presenting month-old data as current.
Continuous
Discovery re-runs; it is not a one-off onboarding exercise
Scans run daily, so a new system, a new data store, a new cloud region, or configuration drift re-opens only the answers it affects, for re-confirmation. The profile the frameworks are scoped against stays the profile the bank actually runs.

How access works

Home
Every major role has a Home, and every working role leads with its queue
Switch role from the user menu in the top bar and Home changes with it: the Executive gets posture and decisions, each working persona gets its own work queue first, Internal Audit gets its testing queue, and the Examiner gets a read-only review home that leads to the proof pack. Underneath the queue, scoped operator roles see only their own slice: the Compliance Analyst Home shows the register, policy, and findings items inside the analyst's assigned scope, not the executive posture panels or the decision actions. The full posture panels stay with the Executive and the CCO.
Sidebar
The Compliance Analyst keeps the full sidebar; limits apply to editing and approval, not navigation
The Compliance Analyst, like the CCO / BSA Officer, sees all major links, including Discovery, Transformation with Growth linked under it, Analytics, Integrations, and Admin with its Personas & Access, BSA/AML Sourcing, and Data Sources & Evidence sub-pages, so no link disappears when the role switches to the Analyst. What the Analyst role limits is editing and approval, not navigation: approving evidence, accepting risk, and signing off the proof pack stay with the roles named in this register. Other working personas see the major links their work needs, and opening a blocked area is refused in the app with a note that points back to this register. The Executive sidebar stays narrowed to its decision view (Home, Pulse, Risk, Reports, Audit), and the Examiner keeps read-only link access with no working sidebar.
Notifications
Notifications follow the role too
The bell in the top bar shows only items assigned to the current persona, items waiting on their approval, and items that escalate to them. Counts change when the role changes; the underlying alerts do not.
Approval
No persona closes its own proof alone
AI can check sufficiency and recommend, but a person approves. Owners cannot approve their own evidence, and risk acceptance always needs a named approver and an end date. Each entry below names the holder, the approver, and the backup or delegate who covers the queue when the holder is away. A backup can triage and prepare work; sign-off stays with the named approver.

BSA/AML Framework Sourcing

Admin · BSA/AML Framework Sourcing · How the base BSA/AML framework is sourced, the references every item traces back to, the taxonomy those sources are built into, and what it takes to keep the baseline current. BSA/AML is a custom framework in Quantarra OS: it is built from public regulatory sources and configured per bank. It is never presented as out of box, and it is never loaded as a bank's approved program.

How the Framework Is Sourced Primary sources only. Nothing in the base framework is vendor-invented, copied from a bank, or taken from a secondary summary.

1Authoritative sourceStatute, regulation, examination manual, or regulator guidance, taken from the publisher's current edition.
›
2ExtractionEach duty is extracted in the source's own terms, with the citation pinned to the section it came from.
›
3NormalizationThe extracted duty is placed in the taxonomy below: domain, obligation, risk, control, evidence, policy link.
›
4SME reviewA BSA/AML subject-matter reviewer checks the extraction and mapping against the source text before release.
›
5Versioned baselineReleased as a dated framework baseline. Banks configure on top of it in Discovery; the baseline itself is never edited in place.
Rule
Every obligation and control carries its citation
If an item cannot be traced to a section of a reference below, it does not enter the baseline. Interpretive material (advisories, typologies) can inform a scenario or a test step, but it never stands in for the regulation text.
Rule
The FFIEC manual is the structural spine, not the law
The taxonomy follows the FFIEC BSA/AML Examination Manual's organization because that is how examiners test. The obligations themselves cite the statute and the FinCEN regulations; where the manual and the regulation differ, the regulation governs and the difference is logged.
Boundary
Sourcing is not legal advice and not a compliance claim
The baseline organizes what the sources require. Whether a specific bank satisfies a requirement is decided by that bank's BSA Officer, its board, and its examiner, using the bank's own configured controls and evidence.

What the Base Framework Contains, and What It Does Not

In the base framework (Quantarra-maintained)
Obligations with citations
Each duty in the source's terms, with its reference, frequency, and regulator date pattern.
The five program pillars as requirements
Internal controls, independent testing, the designated BSA Officer, training, and risk-based CDD, taken from 31 CFR 1020.210(b)(1)–(5) and 12 CFR 326.8(b). The baseline states each pillar as a testable requirement; the bank's own testing reports and training records are the bank's evidence, never baseline content.
The taxonomy and control library
Domains, risks, and control objectives with stable control IDs, ready for a bank to adopt and own.
Evidence requirements
What artifact proves each control, where it normally comes from, and its freshness rule, such as the 30-day rule on BSA extracts.
Policy linkage points
The places where a bank policy section is expected to state each requirement, so the bank's sections can be mapped when it configures.
Draft policy templates, marked as templates
Starting text aligned to the references, clearly labeled as a template. A template becomes a policy only when the bank tailors it and its board approves it; until then it proves nothing.
Transaction monitoring scenario library
Typology-based scenarios drawn from FinCEN material. XYZ's MVP activates 8 of them; each still needs its own implementation pack per control.
Never in the base framework (belongs to the bank)
Approved bank policies
The bank's BSA/AML policies are its own board-approved documents. They enter through Policy Intake in Discovery and are mapped to the linkage points; the framework does not supply them as approved text.
The bank's risk assessment and ratings
Customer, product, and geography risk ratings are the bank's judgments about its own book. The framework supplies the methodology structure (control PG-RA-01), not the ratings.
Thresholds, tuning, and case decisions
Monitoring thresholds, alert dispositions, and SAR filing decisions are bank operations. The framework states what must be documented, not what the bank decided.
Evidence and exam findings
Proof is produced by the bank's systems and people, and findings are raised against the bank. Neither is shipped with the framework.

How the Baseline Is Maintained

Monthly
Source watch
FinCEN, the Federal Register, FFIEC, FDIC, OFAC, and Texas Department of Banking bulletins are checked for new rules, manual updates, advisories, and list-format changes. OFAC list changes are the exception to the monthly rhythm: the SDN and Consolidated Lists change without rulemaking, so list updates are consumed as published and the screening configuration is re-pointed the same day, outside the baseline version cycle. The Obligations Register already runs this watch: sources were last checked Nov 24, 2026, with no new in-scope obligations identified. Every check is logged, including the ones that find nothing.
Event-driven
Change intake and impact assessment
A changed source enters as a candidate change, never as a silent edit. Each candidate is assessed for impact: which obligations, controls, evidence requirements, and policy linkage points it touches, and which bank configurations inherit it.
Per release
Versioned releases with a citation diff
Accepted changes ship as a new dated baseline version with a change log and a citation-level diff against the prior version. Baselines are never edited in place, for the same reason a policy draft is never silently treated as the approved policy.
Per bank
Bank acceptance before anything goes live
A new baseline version does not change a bank's live register on its own. The bank's BSA Officer reviews the diff, accepts it into the bank's configuration in Discovery, and any control whose source changed is flagged for re-mapping and re-proof until its evidence is checked against the new text.
Quarterly
Scenario and typology refresh
The transaction monitoring scenario library is reviewed against new FinCEN advisories and typologies. New scenarios enter as candidates for a bank to activate; retired typologies are deprecated in the baseline, not deleted, so historical dispositions stay interpretable.
Annual
Full re-baseline
Once a year the whole taxonomy is re-checked against the current FFIEC manual edition and 31 CFR Chapter X as published: citations re-pinned, dead references repaired, and domain structure reconciled to the manual. The re-baseline is reviewed by the BSA/AML SME before release, like any other version.
